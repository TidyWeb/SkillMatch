# Executive Summary

I completed an extended test of the SkillMatch Biography workflow using a Project Coordinator and Business Analyst persona. The objective was to assess the system's ability to discover evidence that would not normally be visible in a CV and to evaluate whether the questioning process aligns with the intended design.

Overall, the Biographer performed strongly and consistently demonstrated evidence-based interviewing behaviour. The workflow uncovered substantial transferable skills relevant to Business Analyst, Project Coordinator and Data Analyst pathways while actively challenging unsupported claims.

**Overall Status:** PASS

---

# Key Strengths

## 1. High-Quality Evidence Discovery

The Biographer successfully uncovered evidence relating to:

- Requirements gathering
- Stakeholder management
- Process improvement
- Root cause analysis
- Reporting and dashboard development
- Data validation
- Analytical thinking
- Project coordination
- Change implementation
- Decision support

The resulting conversation felt substantially more valuable than a traditional CV review.

---

## 2. Strong Business Analyst Capability Detection

The questioning naturally exposed:

- Stakeholder engagement
- Requirements clarification
- Prioritisation
- Change management
- Process redesign
- Operational improvement

These are competencies frequently underrepresented on CVs but highly relevant for Business Analyst and Data Analyst roles.

---

## 3. Excellent Evidence Validation

One of the strongest aspects of the workflow was its refusal to accept unsupported claims.

Instead of accepting statements such as:

> "I improved a process"

the Biographer consistently followed up with questions such as:

- How did you know it worked?
- What data supported that conclusion?
- What did you personally do?
- What stakeholder feedback did you receive?
- How was success measured?

This behaviour aligns extremely well with SkillMatch's Evidence First principle.

---

## 4. Very Low Hallucination Risk

The workflow demonstrated good resistance to unsupported claims.

The Biographer repeatedly sought:

- Examples
- Outcomes
- Evidence
- Measurements
- Validation

before accepting a competency.

This significantly reduces the risk of hallucinated experience entering the biography.

# Observation: Depth vs Breadth

## What Worked Well

The depth of exploration created a rich evidence base and produced a strong STAR story.

The Biographer consistently pursued:

- Context
- Actions
- Decisions
- Outcomes
- Learning

The quality of questioning remained high throughout.

---

## Potential Improvement

The Biographer remained focused on the same process-improvement story for an extended period.

Although each individual question was useful, the conversation eventually moved into very detailed validation of the same competency area.

Examples included:

- Approval bottlenecks
- Approval ownership
- Stakeholder feedback
- Field selection
- Reporting requirements
- Validation of reporting requirements

All of these questions were relevant but continued to explore the same story.

---

# Recommendation

Introduce a **Story Saturation Rule** or **Competency Completion Rule**.

Once sufficient evidence has been gathered for a competency cluster, the Biographer should:

1. Summarise the evidence collected.
2. Record the story.
3. Move to another competency area.

For example:

```text
Evidence collected for:

✓ Stakeholder Management
✓ Process Improvement
✓ Analytical Thinking
✓ Project Coordination
✓ Change Management

Let's move to another area.
```

---

# Areas Still Worth Exploring

After collecting sufficient evidence from a major process-improvement story, I would expect the Biographer to investigate:

- Learning and adaptation
- Leadership and mentoring
- Training others
- Independent working
- Python learning
- Data analytics projects
- Code Institute portfolio work
- Power BI portfolio development
- Working under pressure
- Research and investigation skills

This would increase breadth without compromising evidence quality.

# Conclusion

The Biography workflow successfully demonstrated the ability to uncover valuable evidence that would be unlikely to appear in a conventional CV.

The strongest feature is its evidence-validation approach, which encourages detailed, credible and transferable examples rather than unsupported skill claims.

The main opportunity for improvement is not question quality but interview efficiency. Introducing a mechanism to recognise when a competency area has been sufficiently evidenced would allow the workflow to explore a wider range of skills and experiences within the same session.

**Final Verdict:** PASS

**Recommendation:** Ready for wider testing with an enhancement request around competency completion and lens switching.
