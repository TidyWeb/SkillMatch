# My SkillMatch Hackathon Development Diary

## 1. What I thought I was getting into, and how I felt at the start

I went into the hackathon expecting to get more involved in app building using AI. I was looking forward to doing some software engineering. I like new challenges, and I trusted that I would learn new skills using tools such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini. I knew it was going to be stressful, but I was excited to start.

Phil designated me as project manager straight away, because his idea of building a job-finding app for graduates of the course stemmed directly from my original idea: helping people find their dream job in the place they want, and showing them how far they are from achieving their goals.

I expected everyone to contribute to app building, because I thought that was what people normally did during hackathons. And they did!

## 2. What I actually did: the app idea, project management and the data analysis

Phil had previously built a more classical job-finding app, but he added several interesting tools and filters. These included using Code Institute's course experience as a baseline and relying more on distance from home than a typical job app would. There were a lot of filters and tools, and Phil was quite mesmerised by the possibilities! We had to do quite a bit of streamlining and make several front-end modifications.

He gradually moved towards my original idea, although I mostly contributed by asking questions. We had a whole repository and an entire app to understand.

The team worked through a mixture of self-selected roles and Phil creating a safe space for everyone to learn through small tasks and baby steps. I was on board with that approach, because it felt safe while still allowing us to collaborate on a large-scale project.

I allowed Phil to take the lead with GitHub Projects, as he was the most comfortable using it. I was more involved in showing everyone how to use GitHub Colab (especially creating PRs or using Phil's guide to create their own workspace and branches). I then liaised with each team member, made sure everyone was on track, tried to understand everyone's responsibilities, helped where I could and made sure things moved quickly. My project management was largely information- and communication-focused.

The data analytics role was mainly John's. He wanted to understand the job market, its requirements, how those requirements are met, what gaps remain and how to address them. I wanted our app development to be data-driven too, so I asked John to create a Jupyter notebook and then looked at how we could refine it. I helped John identifying the right data sources and polishing the Jupyter notebook on Google Colab, in order to communicate his findings. John produced a full data analytics report, which is available on our website.

I also thought the data analytics could help us change the app's matching algorithm. I therefore investigated how the algorithm worked and, with the help of ChatGPT Projects, implemented a small change on a separate branch.

## 3. What was hard: coordination, GitHub and time

The software engineering side was the most important part of the experience for me, but also the most challenging.

The hardest part, however, was having a new team member join just as the hackathon started and challenge my capability to be project manager. He suggested that I should just be project manager when I did the presentation. It came out of the blue, and when I questioned him, he didn't offer any further explanation. Communication with him was sometimes difficult, particularly when I needed responses to requests for specific tasks. This made coordination more challenging during the initial stages.

The course leader repeatedly asked me, from day one, whether I was up for the challenge of being a project manager, but didn't ask anyone else on the course the same question. I felt that my suitability for the role was being questioned repeatedly in a way that I did not observe happening to other participants. Some subsequent interactions also left me feeling excluded and disrespected, including some very serious allegations and actions. I raised my concerns with Student Care and received understanding and support, as I did from my team members.

In those early moments, I mainly needed my team members to complete some essential tasks quickly, which they did. Things then progressed naturally as we all relaxed more.

Apart from that, the hackathon was a little easier than I expected because we worked quite independently.

I would have liked to be self-appointed rather than simply named as project manager. I would also have liked to use more of the project management tools normally used in a professional setting. However, I don't think that was necessary for this hackathon. The traditional approach of getting things done and keeping track of them on Discord was enough and appropriate.

In retrospect, I could have taken a more active role in ensuring that the GitHub Projects board reflected everyone's progress, rather than relying primarily on Discord communication. However, I think our communication-focused approach was appropriate for the way the team worked.

I learned that project management in a small technical team does not necessarily require extensive formal processes. In our case, regular communication, understanding everyone's responsibilities and helping to remove obstacles were more important than maintaining a detailed project board. At the same time, a project manager needs sufficient visibility of the technical work to identify dependencies, follow progress and make informed decisions. This is something I would approach differently in future projects.

I see the experience as good practice for managing a technology start-up mock-up, almost like a simulation.

## 4. How my ideas changed, and one thing I'd do differently as PM

My personal priority was to investigate the back end and understand how the matching algorithm worked. Phil was very keen to implement certain tools, such as the CV Tailor. Without having software engineering experience myself, I decided to go along with the broader product direction rather than insist that everyone work on the underlying algorithm and improve it.

The new team member also had the idea for such a tool, or at least provided the inspiration for it. I thought it was important for the team to develop a product together and come up with their own ideas, or let the more experienced members do so.

I would have liked a more balanced team, but the junior members didn't seem to mind, so I went along with the arrangement. I hope that was okay! I also got to work on the things that interested me.

If I were managing another hackathon team, I would probably insist more on having a balanced team and making sure team members were more transparent. Phil never explained the repository structure to us, even though he had built it using his AI agent, or possibly multiple AI agents. Given Phil's greater experience with software development and the existing application, I decided to learn from his approach rather than challenge the technical direction without sufficient knowledge of the underlying code. I thought it would be more valuable to learn from him, since he had a good plan.

However, in future projects, I would want greater transparency around the technical architecture and a more balanced distribution of opportunities for team members to contribute.

## 5. How I used AI, and where it helped or got in the way

I used ChatGPT Projects to help me keep track of the entire project, but also DeepSeek to implement direct code changes whenever I ran out of free usage with ChatGPT.

I think DeepSeek did particularly well in terms of the code, whereas ChatGPT was definitely more suited to helping me maintain a professional outlook on the project.

AI does tend to rush into giving you answers, which means you sometimes have to trace things back several times. I had to double-check everything against the actual code, documentation and other project materials. But that was fine by me! I didn't want to blindly do what AI told me.

The experience also helped me make up my mind about my preferred career direction. I would continue pursuing data analytics and project management roles, and of course improve my skills in those areas. The experience helped me clarify that software engineering is not the career direction I want to pursue.

Nonetheless, it was a good challenge. I enjoy regular coding challenges because they help me stay up to date with new technology developments and use code intelligently in my work. I will always need to understand algorithms, and AI and machine learning are two areas I might still consider for future work.

## 6. What it's done for me: skills, confidence and what's next

Despite the difficulties, the experience made me feel more confident about my project management skills. Feedback from others would still be useful, but the feedback I've received from team members so far has only been positive. I also welcomed the negative challenges, because they gave me something to work through.

I think I will feel less stressed in future projects. I need to practise having my authority respected more naturally. I sometimes worry that people might disregard me because I'm a woman or because I look too childish.

I will be using SkillMatch's tools, although perhaps not immediately, as I have a flurry of job applications to complete as required by my course.

I do want to continue developing the app. The matching algorithm needs improving, as does the user interface. I'd like to make the interface more minimalistic and closer to my own taste. Everything I need to work with is already there.

Before taking the project further, however, the team would need to clarify ownership, intellectual property rights, attribution and potential commercial arrangements. My vision for the app is quite different from Phil's, so we would also need to establish whether we share the same longer-term objectives. The website credits are currently mainly attributed to Phil's GitHub profile because he built the current application, even though all team members are listed.

These are not immediate concerns, but they would need to be addressed before any substantial further development or commercialisation. For now, I have other priorities, including my job applications, but I remain interested in seeing how far SkillMatch could go.
