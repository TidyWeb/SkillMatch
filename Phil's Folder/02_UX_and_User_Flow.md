# Task B — UX and user flow

## Purpose

Make it obvious how a graduate moves from their situation to a useful, explainable shortlist. The task is to answer practical design questions with a rough wireframe and feedback, not to polish final graphics.

**Owner:** __________  **Reviewer:** __________  **Rotating hat today:** __________

## User and core task

Design for a Code Institute Data Analytics with AI graduate looking for a first or early-career role. They want to answer:

> “Which of these roles might fit my skills and circumstances, why did they appear, and what would I need to check next?”

Include West Midlands work locations and UK remote roles only when eligibility is evidenced. Do not assume every candidate drives, lives in a particular place, or wants to share personal information.

## Explore these questions

- What must the candidate tell the app before useful results can appear?
- Should the experience be one page with sections or a short sequence of views? Could someone understand it without opening several browser windows?
- How should the page distinguish course coverage, candidate-confirmed skills, job requirements, and missing evidence?
- How should location, remote eligibility, hybrid office attendance, pay, experience, and unknown details be shown?
- What is the smallest explanation that makes a match trustworthy without pretending to be a hiring decision?
- What must work on a narrow phone screen, even if the hackathon demo is desktop-first?

## Suggested starting flow to test

Use one responsive browser app with three clear states or sections; change this if feedback shows a better route.

1. **Profile and preferences:** select or confirm skills, optionally describe experience/qualifications, and set the preferred area or travel constraint. Clearly label course topics as coverage, not proof of proficiency. Avoid accounts, CV upload, and saving real personal details in the first demo.
2. **Shortlist:** show role title, employer, location/arrangement, early-career judgement, advertised pay or “not stated”, and a concise match summary. Let a user see why it passed or why it needs review.
3. **Role detail:** show evidence for required and desirable skills, gaps, experience and qualification requirements, remote/hybrid eligibility, source link, checked date, and unresolved facts.

## Make a low-cost prototype

- Sketch the three states on paper, a shared board, or a simple static wireframe.
- Use one sample candidate and three contrasting vacancy examples: a plausible entry role, an adjacent/stretch role, and a role that should be excluded or labelled unresolved.
- Ask two or three people to try the same tasks, if available. Do not ask them to share real contact details, CVs, or home addresses.
- Give each person these tasks without explaining the answer: find a plausible role; explain one match and one gap; identify a hybrid or remote constraint; tell you what to do if the pay or eligibility is unknown.
- Record where they hesitate or misread labels. This is directional feedback, not representative user research.

## Deliver to the team

- One journey sketch showing the user's steps and decision points.
- One rough screen/wireframe for the shortlist and one detail view.
- Brief notes from the task try-outs: what people understood, where they got stuck, and the change recommended.
- A short “must show / can wait” list to protect the build scope.

## UX acceptance checks

- [ ] The app clearly identifies the source and checked date for a result.
- [ ] Match reasons, missing skills, and uncertainty can be understood without an unexplained percentage.
- [ ] Candidate-confirmed skills are visually distinct from course coverage.
- [ ] Early-career suitability is explained by the advert evidence, not title alone.
- [ ] West Midlands location and UK remote eligibility are not conflated.
- [ ] Unknown salary, travel, or eligibility is shown as unknown rather than guessed.
- [ ] The core task works in one simple flow, and important content remains readable on a phone-width screen.
- [ ] The prototype does not require a real CV, account, or stored home postcode.
