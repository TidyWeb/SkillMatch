# SkillMatch Hackathon – Additional Reflection on Team Dynamics and Contributions

## Project coordination and communication

One aspect of the hackathon that I feel deserves further explanation is my contribution to managing team discussions and maintaining momentum.

Although Phil took the lead on the technical development and often led discussions about the application, I frequently found myself having to manage the communication around those discussions. This involved identifying the most important points, summarising lengthy explanations, bringing conversations back to the immediate objectives and, when necessary, interrupting discussions so that the team could move on to implementation.

This was particularly important because we had only four days to develop the project. I was conscious that lengthy discussions could consume valuable working time, leave team members tired and prevent us from completing the tasks we had agreed upon.

My approach was therefore to encourage practical decisions, keep discussions focused and ensure that people had enough time to carry out their work. I see this as an important part of my project management contribution, even though it was less visible than the technical development itself.

## Differences in how the team experience has been represented

I have subsequently read an account of the team's experience written by another team member. While I recognise that everyone is entitled to their own interpretation of events, I was disappointed by the way certain interactions and their resolution were represented.

In particular, the account describes the difficulties following SK's arrival and attributes their resolution primarily to the technical demonstrations, project reviews and a subsequent individual discussion. It does not adequately acknowledge the communication and coordination work that I was undertaking during this period.

My experience was that managing discussions, condensing information and keeping the team focused on practical work were essential to making progress. I do not think it is reasonable for an account of the team's development to present the resolution of these difficulties without acknowledging that contribution.

I also felt uncomfortable that an account of the team's experience could be written and potentially used to represent our collective work without first giving the other people involved an opportunity to contribute their perspectives. I would have preferred a more transparent and collaborative retrospective, in which everyone's experiences and contributions could be represented.

I cannot determine another person's intentions from their account alone, but I do not consider this representation sufficiently balanced to reflect my experience of the hackathon.

## Trust and future collaboration

This has changed how I feel about continuing to develop SkillMatch collaboratively.

I initially saw the hackathon as the beginning of something that the team could potentially develop further. I was interested in improving the matching algorithm, refining the product and exploring its longer-term potential.

However, collaboration requires more than technical ability. It also requires transparency, mutual respect, recognition of contributions and trust. I would find it difficult to continue working on a project where I felt that these principles were not being respected.

For now, I have decided to reconsider my involvement in any future collaborative development. This does not diminish the value of what we achieved during the hackathon, nor does it mean that I have lost interest in the underlying idea. It means that I need to be more careful about the working relationships and arrangements under which I contribute to future projects.
