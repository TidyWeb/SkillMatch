# Decisions

Choices made while building, with the reason. Newest at the bottom of each section.

## Tooling

- **uv with a hatchling build backend, Python pinned to 3.12.** The project installs as an editable package so `python -m skillmatch.*` works from anywhere in the repo.
- **Notebook dependencies are a separate uv group (`notebook`).** The web image and the default `make install` stay small; `uv sync --group notebook` adds Jupyter, SciPy and Matplotlib.
- **`reports/` is git-ignored.** Quality reports are build artefacts of a particular data run; the ETL regenerates them.

## Sources and the live sample (milestone 2)

- **Reed documents no rate limit, attribution rule or storage terms.** We self-limit (1 request per second, 1,500 per run, back-off, 20-hour disk cache), credit "Jobs from Reed.co.uk", link every card to reed.co.uk, show only short evidence sentences rather than full descriptions, and never commit vacancy data. See docs/sources.md.
- **Training-course adverts are excluded** with `excluded_reason = "training course, not a job"`. 10% of the live sample were paid courses posing as "Trainee Data Analyst" jobs. They would otherwise be the top matches for our users.
- **Reed search-row postcodes are kept.** About 40% of search rows give a full postcode where the detail record gives only a town; it geocodes more precisely.
- **Implied data-experience demand.** "Strong/proven experience as a data analyst" with no number counts as a data-experience demand of 1 year, flagged as implied. Only 9 of 162 real adverts give an explicit number of years.
- **Desirable cues beat section headings.** A sentence ending "…would be desirable" under an "About you" heading is desirable. A leading "ideally" makes it desirable; a mid-sentence "ideally" after an essential cue does not.
- **Near-duplicates may span towns when the employer is the same and the text is near-identical.** Agencies post one role in many towns. This extends the brief's same-title-and-town rule.

## Taxonomy (milestone 3)

- **Sector knowledge is modelled as competencies (category `sector`).** Real adverts often make sector experience essential ("must have automotive experience"). A nurse's NHS knowledge or a store manager's retail knowledge then shows as met instead of an unexplained gap.
- **Added competencies the live adverts use often:** analytical thinking, clear communication, customer service, numerical confidence, adaptability and learning, teamwork, organisation and prioritising, following procedures.
- **A tiny phrase language for reviewers:** `*` lets a word continue, and `...` allows up to four words between. Matching ignores capitals and works on whole words. Ambiguous English words ("Excel", "Sage") match only when capitalised (`variants_cs`); "R" and "SQL" use regexes so "R&D" and "T-SQL" don't trigger them.
- **Added skills not in the brief's list:** business analysis and APIs/JSON. Many real adverts in the sample were business-analyst roles.
- **Validation reports every problem at once**, naming the file and entry, and rejects unknown fields to catch typos. `python -m skillmatch.taxonomy validate` runs it standalone.

## Fixtures (milestone 4)

- **Fixtures are generated by `scripts/make_fixtures.py` and committed as JSON.** The blueprints are readable prose, so reviewers can edit an advert and regenerate. All employers are fictional, `source = "fixture"`, and URLs point at example.org.
- **Five advert "styles" copy the shapes seen on Reed**, not their text: agency header plus lists; specialist recruiter with `*` bullets in plain text; council/charity essential and desirable criteria; heading-free prose; hard-wrapped `•` lines.
- **As on Reed, agency-posted fixtures carry the agency's name as the employer.** Near-duplicates are the same role re-posted by a second agency; cross-source duplicates are the same role on a second board.
- **Fixtures include deliberate traps:** training-course adverts, non-data roles, senior titles, out-of-region towns (Cheltenham, Leicester), an unresolvable location, a region-only location and an expired advert.

## ETL (milestone 5)

- **Reed is filtered twice.** Search rows go through the training-course, region, title and relevance filters (on the title and Reed's short description snippet) before any details call. Survivors are re-checked on the full description. A data role that only shows its skills beyond the snippet can be lost; titles catch most of them.
- **Exclusion precedence:** training course > remote role not open to UK applicants > outside the West Midlands > senior role > not a data role. The senior title word is recorded separately (`title_excluded_word`) even when another reason wins, so insights can count by word.
- **Relevance rule:** a data word in the title, or at least 2 distinct *non-generic* technical skills in the description. Excel, Microsoft Office, Google Sheets, ERP and HR systems don't count, because almost every office advert mentions them. The threshold is `config.RELEVANCE_MIN_SKILLS`.
- **Remote:** a job is remote when its location is a remote marker ("UK-remote", "Remote", "Home based"), it comes from a remote board, or its location is just "United Kingdom" and the advert says remote. Remote roles explicitly closed to UK applicants are excluded. Unclear eligibility is kept and labelled.
- **Remote OK salaries are never shown** (they may be platform estimates). Free-text salaries are parsed only when the period is stated or the figure is unambiguously annual (≥ £10,000).
- **Only essential statements create a data-experience demand.** "Ideally 1–2 years" never gates. Ranges use the lower bound; "several" counts as 3.
- **Stored text:** only requirement and duty sentences (≤ 400 characters each) are stored as evidence, never whole descriptions.
- **Workable:** we use the endpoint in Workable's own help article (`www.workable.com/api/accounts/{subdomain}?details=true`), not the widget URL the brief mentions (see docs/sources.md).
- **The profile's postcode is cut to the outward code in the browser.** The server never sees the full postcode when JavaScript is on, and reduces it on arrival when it isn't.

## Matching (milestone 6)

- **Several tags in one requirement:** with "or", "such as" or "e.g." the best tag counts. Otherwise all met = met, all gaps = gap, anything else = partly met. A requirement listing three things the user half has is partly met, not a gap.
- **"Interest in [sector]" is not sector experience:** sector tags are dropped from requirements that ask only for an interest.
- **An advert with no readable essential requirements is never a Strong fit** (at most Good fit), with a reason telling the user to read it in full.
- **A data-experience gap of up to a year with no entry signals** counts as one essential gap (Good fit at best) rather than being ignored.
- **Data-experience gaps round up to whole years** (1.5 → 2 → Stretch).
- **The office-days preference and minimum salary act as filters**, with hidden counts and a reveal, like the senior-roles filter. Unstated salaries are never hidden by the salary filter.
- **Matcher test expectations were worked out by hand first.** 47 of 48 agreed with the code. In the one disagreement the matcher was judged fairer: the ex-chef "partly" meets "accurate data entry and record keeping" through evidenced attention to detail. That expectation was updated with a comment.

## Calibration on live data (milestone 7, 21 October 2026)

Snapshot: 2,790 unique adverts (Reed 2,565 search results across a 16-town grid × 23 terms; Greenhouse, Lever and Workable boards 219; We Work Remotely 13; Remote OK 9). The calibration script is `scripts/calibrate.py`; its full reports (git-ignored) include 20 random persona × advert matches read end to end.

### Before (rules as built in milestones 5–6)

- Shown by default: 81. Excluded: not a data role 1,068, outside the West Midlands 867, senior role 699, training course 64.
- Tier spread per persona (Strong / Good / Stretch / Not yet): PhD 7/38/17/17 · chef 6/32/19/24 · director 7/30/17/18 · nurse 5/32/18/26 · store manager 4/21/14/30 · teacher 6/36/19/20.
- Requirements: 23% unclassified (169 of 727), 15% inferred. 10 of 81 adverts had no tagged essential requirement.
- The first live run hit Reed's 1,500-request cap after only 72 detail calls, because postcode lookups shared Reed's request budget.

### What reading the matches showed, and the fixes

1. **Request budget:** postcode lookups shared Reed's cap. → Separate budgets per provider. The search-stage region check uses only the offline tables, and clearly out-of-region postcode areas (GL, LE, NN…) are skipped before any lookup.
2. **The relevance rule cut real analysis jobs** our users could do ("Operational Performance Analyst", "Commercial Analyst", pricing, supply-chain and planning analysts) **and kept non-data jobs** ("Information Security Analyst", "Backend Engineer", "Automation Engineer", "R&D Intern", "Management Reporting Accountant"). → New rule in `filters.is_data_role`: a data word in the title, unless the title also names a non-data field; "<qualifier> Analyst" unless the qualifier is finance, business, IT support, security, SAP, PMO and similar; otherwise 2+ non-generic skills, but never for engineer, developer and similar titles. "Database" now counts as a data word.
3. **Repeated adverts:** some posters paste their requirements twice. → Repeated statements within an advert are kept once.
4. **Headings run into the text** ("…specialist area What's On Offer?") made benefits count as essential requirements. → Known benefit, company and apply headings are split off. Long run-together headings ending in ":" (up to 14 words) count as headings. "If You've Got It - We Want It" and "What you have" are requirement headings. "Bonus:" at the start of a line means desirable.
5. **"8+ years in business analytics"** (no word "experience") wasn't a data-experience demand. → "N years in/of/as…" counts when a data anchor is nearby.
6. **A course project rating met "practical/proven/hands-on experience" requirements.** → For statements that demand work experience, a skill "used in a project" is partly met, with its own explanation. "Confident" and evidenced competencies still meet them.
7. **False tags:** "hospitality" matched the health-sector phrase "hospital*"; "data warehousing" matched warehouse work; "administration" signalled data entry; "data transformation" signalled change management; "learn new systems" signalled change management; "professional services" signalled finance. → Phrases tightened, plus a new optional `not_phrases` field so reviewers can veto a false trigger.
8. **Missing phrases** for common real wording: executive-level reporting, presenting data, customer lifecycle/retention/value, commercial insights, datasets, "analysing … data", messy/incomplete data, taking initiative, leadership teams. → Added.
9. **More boilerplate:** "use your preferred name to apply", "if you thrive on…", "career changers … are encouraged to apply", "competitive salary", "benefits package". → Treated as not requirements.
10. **The demo personas were too sparse.** The nurse had no communication or stakeholder skills ticked, so every "excellent communication skills" requirement was a gap, which a recruiter would never accept. → Each persona now evidences the everyday competencies people in that career really have. The archetype lists suggest them too, and the director's minimum salary is £30,000 rather than £35,000. The matcher test expectations changed accordingly; each change was re-derived by hand and is explained in `tests/match_cases.py`.
11. **Sentence wording:** skill labels that start with an ordinary word are lower-cased mid-sentence ("you have used data cleaning and validation in a project").

### After

- Shown by default: 77. Excluded: not a data role 1,072, outside the West Midlands 867, senior role 699, training course 64. Duplicates: 356 records grouped under another advert.
- Tier spread per persona (Strong / Good / Stretch / Not yet): PhD 16/35/10/15 · chef 11/39/15/12 · director 13/34/13/13 · nurse 13/35/16/13 · store manager 12/30/14/9 · teacher 13/31/20/13. No persona has nearly everything in one group, and each has a different mix.
- Requirements: 17% unclassified (115 of 676), 16% inferred. 5 of 77 adverts have no tagged essential requirement.
- Senior words hidden: manager 549, senior 202, lead 72, head of 70, director 40, chief 11, architect 10, principal 4, staff 2.

### Known limits left after calibration

- Good fit is the biggest group for everyone. The brief's rule ("at most one essential gap") lets an advert with several partly-met requirements and one gap be a Good fit. We kept the brief's rule; the reasons list shows the partly-met items.
- The live West Midlands supply is small: 77 data adverts after filtering one night's Reed, ATS and remote feeds. Many adverts are agency reposts.
- Software-adjacent titles (developer, engineer) are excluded unless they carry a data word. A "Power Platform Developer" doing reporting work is therefore hidden; users can reveal non-data roles.

## Pages (milestones 8–9)

- **The profile never touches the server's disk.** It lives in sessionStorage and is posted with each HTMX request (`data-send-profile`). There's no Flask session or cookie, and a test checks that a marker in a posted profile never appears in any file.
- **Stretch and Not yet groups are collapsed by default**, so the results page leads with what's worth applying for. The tier is shown in words plus three pips (filled pips = better fit), never by colour alone.
- **"How your background answers this advert" lists met requirements only**, as the brief asks. Partly-met lines stay in the requirement table.
- **Charts:** one series per chart, so one colour (the site's blue). Horizontal bars with wrapped labels, sized from the longest label so nothing overlaps at 380px. Every chart states n and the data mode and has a "Show as a table" twin. Headline figures are stat tiles, not one-bar charts. No percentages anywhere: shares are written as "k of n".
- **Map:** markers are coloured by fit using a validated one-hue ramp (Not yet #94acd0 → Stretch #6a8dc0 → Good #4470ad → Strong #1f4e8c), with larger markers for better fits, a legend naming each shade, and OpenStreetMap attribution. Adverts that share a town centre are spread into a small ring (at most about half a mile) so each can be clicked; the caption says so.

## Analysis notebook (milestone 10)

- **Generated from `scripts/build_notebook.py`** so the analysis is reviewable as code and rebuildable. `--execute` runs it against the current database. Committed outputs are aggregate counts and test results only, with no advert text.
- **Test choice is automatic:** chi-square when every expected count is at least 5, otherwise Fisher's exact. Both hypotheses used Fisher's exact on the 21 October snapshot.
- **Bug found and fixed:** pandas reads a missing data-experience demand as NaN, and `entry_plausible` treated only `None` as missing, so the notebook found 1 entry-plausible advert instead of 70. Fixed, with a test.

## Container and deployment (milestone 11)

- **The runtime image installs only the web dependencies.** Flask, PyYAML, RapidFuzz and Gunicorn are the project dependencies. pandas, requests, BeautifulSoup and python-dotenv are a uv `etl` group, installed by default locally (`[tool.uv] default-groups`) but not in the image. A test blocks those modules and checks every page still renders. The image went from 296 MB to 151 MB and is ready in about 0.9 s.
- **`SKILLMATCH_ROOT`** tells an installed (non-editable) package where `data/` is. The image sets it to `/app`.
- **Gunicorn runs one worker with 8 threads and `--preload`.** Memory stays low, the taxonomy compiles once, and Cloud Run's maximum of one instance is enough for a hackathon demo.
- **Docker's daemon wasn't running on the build machine**, and starting it needs root. The image was built and run with rootless Podman instead (`make docker-build DOCKER=podman`); the Dockerfile is standard. GitHub Actions builds with Docker.
- **The nightly workflow fails fast if `REED_API_KEY` is missing.** Otherwise it would deploy a database without Reed's adverts.
- **A separate `ci.yml`** lints and tests on every push using fixtures only, so no secrets are needed for pull requests.

## Self-check fixes (milestone 12)

The self-check (`scripts/self_check.py`) renders /results, /insights and three job pages per persona in fixtures and live mode, and flags "None", "nan", raw HTML, unfilled placeholders, empty lists and broken internal links. It ends clean in both modes. Reading the saved page text found the following, now fixed:

- **"Experience required:" wasn't a requirements heading**, so a Power BI Developer advert's whole list was "inferred" and a nurse came out a Strong fit. Headings containing "required", or starting with Experience, Skills, Knowledge or Qualifications, are now requirement headings.
- **A Strong fit now needs at least two essential requirements to check:** stated ones, plus inferred ones the user fully meets (inferred requirements may raise a match, never sink one; `config.STRONG_MIN_ESSENTIALS`). Adverts that put almost everything under "Ideally…" no longer come out Strong on a single requirement; the reasons say why.
- **The answers list** no longer repeats a competency met twice (normal and leadership wording). "Also listed by" names the other agency, or says "another advert" for a repost on the same source. "None stated" became "Not asked for".

Final live tier spread (21 October snapshot, Strong / Good / Stretch / Not yet): PhD 15/35/10/16 · chef 8/42/14/13 · director 11/36/12/14 · nurse 11/37/15/14 · store manager 10/32/14/9 · teacher 11/33/19/14. Unclassified requirements: 17%; inferred: 15%.

## Ranking (29 October 2026)

### Prioritise match tier over distance

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** The original shortlist ordering prioritised distance. That could place a nearby Good fit or Stretch role above a more relevant Strong fit role, despite matching skills to roles being the app's primary purpose.

**Decision:** Sort vacancies by match tier first (Strong, Good, Stretch, Not yet), then by distance within each tier, and finally by title alphabetically. The rationale is documented in [CHANGE_Justification_Tier_First_Ranking.md](docs/CHANGE_Justification_Tier_First_Ranking.md).

**Consequences:**
- Users see the most relevant matches first; distance remains useful for ordering roles within the same tier.
- Roles with unknown distances are not disadvantaged against lower-tier roles, though known distances still rank first within a tier.
- Users may need to scroll farther for a nearby role when higher-tier matches are farther away.
- The change is limited to result presentation; match-tier calculation and thresholds are unchanged. `tests/test_ranking.py` covers the ordering contract and tie-breakers.

## Relevance judged on the title only (29 October 2026)

This replaces the relevance rule above (2+ non-generic skills in the description).

- **Why:** a Video Editor advert at PlanetScale showed as a match for a chef. PlanetScale sells databases, so its company blurb mentions MySQL and PostgreSQL, and the old rule counted those as the job's skills.
- **Evidence:** on the 27 October live run, the skills-in-description route let in 98 adverts under 59 titles. None was an entry-level data job: Scrum Master (32), account executives, product managers, risk managers, customer support, a full-stack developer advertised in Spanish, the Video Editor. Every real data job in the run already passed on its title.
- **New rule:** a data word in the title, or "<qualifier> Analyst", with the same non-data exceptions as before. Anything else is "not a data role".
- **Old databases:** the app rechecks relevance on the title when it reads a database, so snapshots built before this change (including the Codespaces live-data release) drop the same adverts. The SQLite files are unchanged.
- **Effect:** the three test profiles go from 86 shown jobs to 77; only those nine go.
- **Risk:** a real data job with an unusual title and no data word in it would now be hidden. It stays countable as "not a data role". If one turns up, add its title word to `DATA_TITLE` rather than bringing the description route back.
