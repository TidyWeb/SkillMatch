A working plan for a graduate-focused job matching app

Project plan / current direction

Graduate Job-Matching App

A focused search for early-career data and insight roles that fit a graduate’s skills and real journey to work.

For

Code Institute Data Analytics with AI graduates looking for a first or early-career role.

Working direction · 16 October 2026
Update, 23 October 2026

This plan dates from 16 October. Since then the interface has changed from Streamlit to a Flask web app, and a working prototype exists. Everything else below still describes the plan. Latest news is on the team hub.

Direction on 16 October

Streamlit interface · local Python parsing rules · no paid LLM API · West Midlands plus clearly eligible UK remote roles.

01 / the route

From vacancy to useful recommendation

1

Collect and prepare

  • Use sources with an authorised API, feed or other permitted access route.
  • Keep the source, advert link, retrieval date and available job fields.
  • Normalize records, identify duplicates and mark stale or closed adverts.

Record produced

One traceable, current vacancy record per advert.

2

Parse the advert Phase 2 focus

  • Normalize text; match skill aliases with a controlled vocabulary and local Python rules.
  • Extract pay, title level, required experience, qualifications, skills and work location.
  • Label skill mentions required, desirable, incidental, negated or unclear where possible.
  • Keep the supporting sentence; send ambiguous wording to review instead of guessing.

Evidence produced

Structured facts linked to the exact text that supports them.

3

Assess early-career suitability

  • Classify each advert as a plausible entry route, adjacent/stretch, experienced/senior or unresolved.
  • Record stated experience and qualifications; keep borderline or unclear wording visible for review.
  • Use title and advert context together; “senior stakeholders” alone does not make a role senior.
  • Preserve advertised pay, pay period and pro-rata details; keep missing pay unknown and do not estimate it.
  • Set the final salary policy only after the team resolves the £30,000 question.

Assessment produced

An explained entry-route category, with unknowns kept visible.

4

Compare with the candidate

  • Seed the profile with course topics, labelled as course coverage rather than proven proficiency.
  • Let the candidate confirm confidence and add skills, qualifications and experience.
  • Compare confirmed skills with required and desirable advert skills; show evidence and gaps.
  • Use clear outcomes such as Good fit, Possible fit and Excluded; avoid an unexplained percentage.

Fit explanation

What matched, what is missing and which advert wording led to that conclusion.

5

Check the journey and present results

  • Use the candidate’s home postcode and chosen travel limit.
  • Ignore commute distance for remote work; account for office days in hybrid roles.
  • Add car or public-transport time and cost only if a suitable routing source is available.
  • Show the source advert, suitability reasons and important unknowns.

Remaining choice

Routing provider, travel modes and travel-limit controls are not selected yet.

02 / analysis

What the collected jobs can tell us

  • Which skills recur in plausible entry-route adverts.
  • How adverts fall across the early-career categories, and why.
  • Which sources add unique suitable vacancies.
  • How often salary, experience, location or work pattern is missing.
  • How commute limits change the reachable opportunities.

Report these as findings about the collected sample, not the whole job market unless source coverage supports that claim.

03 / team decisions

Still to settle

  • Which sources permit collection, display, storage and analysis.
  • Live feed, reviewed snapshot, or both for the prototype.
  • Salary policy: hard £30,000 ceiling, candidate-set filter, or display-only. Current source research has no mandatory cutoff.
  • Routing method and treatment of non-annual or pro-rata pay.
  • Candidate and vacancy data-retention approach.
  • Final experience threshold for borderline roles.

Source research

  • Vacancy source inventory
  • Search method and coverage
