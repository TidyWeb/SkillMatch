# SkillMatch Presentation Script (4 Minutes Total)

## Part 1: Introduction (Oana) - 2 Minutes
*   **The Problem:** Imagine finishing the Code Institute Data Analytics course and facing a "blizzard" of online job adverts. There are hundreds of opportunities, but many aren't aimed at your specific situation.
*   **Our Audience:** This is highly relevant to our cohort, as most of us are career changers. We have years of transferable skills and professional experience, but not necessarily years in a *data* role.
*   **The Core Question:** How can we help people recognise the value of their existing experience, rather than just matching them to jobs based on previous job titles?
*   **The Origin:** The idea started as a joke in our Discord chat about finding your dream job and seeing how far you are from it. Phil built a prototype a week before the hackathon, giving us a foundation to build upon.
*   **The Solution:** SkillMatch uses the skills taught on the Code Institute course as a baseline. It looks beyond job titles to identify transferable skills and relevant opportunities, and highlights where users might need to develop further.
*   **Accessibility:** It’s free to use, requires no login, and initially focuses on the West Midlands.
*   **Project Scope:** What started as a job-finding app evolved into five connected elements: the SkillMatch app, a GitHub guide, a LinkedIn guide, a 'Tailor My CV' tool, and our Data Analysis of the job market.
*   **The Team:** Built by a team of five during the Code Institute hackathon (26-30 October), combining different skills to create something genuinely useful.
*   **Handover:** "And now, [Your Name] will take you through the data analysis of the job market."

## Part 2: Data Analytics Section (Your Name) - 2 Minutes

### Slide 1: The Overview (30 seconds)
*   **The Goal:** We looked at one captured week of job adverts for SkillMatch to see if the skills the course teaches match what employers actually want.
*   **The Headline:** We start with a massive funnel: **24,656 search appearances** led to **4,654 distinct adverts**, which eventually narrowed down to just **67 jobs matched to a graduate**.

### Slide 2: The Haystack (45 seconds)
*   **The Visual:** To visualize this, the dashboard uses 'The Haystack'—one dot per advert. It shows the blizzard of noise.
*   **The Funnel:** We start with 4,654 dots. We filter for 'Data Jobs' and drop to 1,988. 'Near Me' gets us to 1,512. 'Entry Level' slices it down to just 85.
*   **The Result:** Finally, we see **67 coral dots** glowing. It's a powerful visual: most of the blizzard was never meant for a graduate. The funnel is the story, not the total.

### Slide 3: Who Advertises? (30 seconds)
*   **Agencies Dominate:** Looking at those 67 matched jobs, agencies placed 45 of them.
*   **Direct Employers:** Strikingly, **zero** of the 67 jobs were confirmed as posted directly by the employer. The rest are 'Unclear.'
*   **The Transparency Gap:** Direct employers hide pay 72% of the time, while agencies hide it only 12% of the time.

### Slide 4: The Takeaway & Limitations (15 seconds)
*   **The AI Caveat:** A quick note on our data: the advertiser labels were AI-generated and human-checked. The AI over-guessed direct employers, which taught us that we can't fully trust automated labelling without a human check.
*   **Conclusion:** Ultimately, matching isn't about casting a wide net; it's about aggressive elimination to find that perfect 67.
